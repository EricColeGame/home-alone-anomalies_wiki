The premise of [Home Alone: Anomalies](https://www.roblox.com/games/87468080405188/Home-Alone-Anomalies) is simple enough to explain in one sentence and stressful enough to keep you second-guessing every knock. You are home alone. Visitors keep arriving at the door. Not everyone is who they claim to be, and the game asks you to sort the genuine from the deceptive before the night ends. That gap between what a visitor says and what a visitor is forms the core of the experience, and it is why the game has built a large audience since its release.

This article separates what the official Roblox page actually documents from what players report anecdotally, and it flags plainly where the record is silent so you can verify things yourself instead of trusting a guess.

## What the Official Page Confirms

The official Roblox listing, created by the groups 3 AM Productions and Freeground, describes a loop built on observation and judgment. According to that page, players are expected to:

- Check cameras to gather information about what is happening outside
- Listen carefully to what visitors say
- Open the door when a visitor genuinely needs help
- Turn off the porch light when something seems off
- Survive the night, which earns cash
- Use troll items on friends

The place was created on May 4, 2026, and was last updated October 10, 2026. It supports up to 18 players per server and has accumulated roughly 208 million visits as of the official record retrieved October 14, 2026. Those numbers alone tell you the game found an audience quickly, but they say nothing about how any specific visitor behaves.

That distinction matters. The official page tells you the decisions you will make. It does not publish a master list of visitors, a catalogue of anomaly types, a house layout, or a set of endings. If you see a guide claiming to have the complete, developer-confirmed roster, treat it as community work rather than documentation.

## Why Judgment Beats Memorization

Anomaly-style games often attract players who want a lookup table: see X, do Y. Home Alone: Anomalies resists that approach somewhat because the official description frames the experience around listening and evaluating rather than pattern-matching a fixed list. You are told to listen to what visitors say and to decide who to trust, which implies that the information you need arrives through the game itself, not through a wiki.

The practical consequence is that your first few nights should be spent gathering your own evidence. Pay attention to what a visitor claims, compare it against what the cameras show, and note when those two things disagree. Because the official page documents cameras and a porch light as your tools, those are the levers to test first. Turn the porch light off when a visitor's story does not hold up, and observe what changes. Whether the game rewards or punishes that choice in a given situation is something you confirm in your own session, not something the official record specifies.

## A Reasonable Way to Approach a Night

Because the official page does not give you a decision tree, build your own routine. Start by establishing a baseline: use the cameras early, before the first knock, so you know what normal looks like on your street. When a visitor arrives, listen to the full line of dialogue instead of reacting to the first words. Then compare the claim to the camera feed. If the two line up and the visitor appears to need help, the official description supports opening the door. If something is off, the porch light is your documented tool for responding to that feeling.

Two process notes are worth keeping in mind. First, this is an 18-player experience per the official record, so if you are playing in a public server, other players and the troll items mentioned on the page can affect what you see. Do not assume every strange event is an anomaly; some of it may be another player. Second, cash from surviving the night is described as a reward for success, not a currency with published prices, so treat any guide quoting exact amounts as unverified.
