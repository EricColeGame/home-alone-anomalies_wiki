Most Roblox horror games ask you to run from something. Home Alone Anomalies asks you to stand at a door and make a judgment call. The premise is simple enough to explain in one sentence: you are home alone, visitors keep showing up, and not every visitor is telling the truth about who they are. What makes the experience interesting is not jump scares but the small decisions you make before you ever touch the doorknob.

This article sticks to what the official Roblox page and reputable third-party guides actually document, separates that from community chatter, and is honest about the parts of the game nobody has published reliable information on.

## What Home Alone Anomalies Is, According to the Official Page

The experience is published under place ID 87468080405188 by the groups [3 AM Productions and Freeground](https://www.roblox.com/games/87468080405188/Home-Alone-Anomalies). The official record shows the place was created on May 4, 2026, last updated October 10, 2026, supports up to 18 players per server, and has accumulated roughly 208 million visits. It is a separate game from Anomaly Watch and from other anomaly-spotting titles that share similar keywords — a distinction worth remembering, because search results for "anomaly" games blur together quickly.

The official description frames the core loop directly: you are home alone, visitors arrive at the door, and not everyone is who they claim to be. Advertised features include checking cameras, listening to what visitors say, opening the door when someone genuinely needs help, turning off the porch light when something feels off, and surviving the night to earn cash. The page also mentions troll items designed for messing with friends, which tells you the developers expect private servers and group play, not just solo runs.

## The Decision Loop: Door, Light, Camera, Listen

The four advertised verbs — check, listen, open, turn off — describe a decision loop rather than a checklist. Based on how the official description and third-party guides frame it, a typical visitor encounter works roughly like this:

1. Someone arrives and speaks. The dialogue is information, not decoration. What they say is one of your inputs.
2. You can check the camera feed for visual context the door itself does not give you.
3. You decide whether the visitor's story holds together.
4. If they need help, the official page says opening the door is the correct move.
5. If something is off, the official page says turning off the porch light is the response.

The important detail is that the official description treats the porch light as a tool, not a toggle you leave alone. It is an action you take when your read on a visitor is wrong. Community guides generally describe the same rhythm, but they are third-party observations rather than developer documentation, so treat their specific visitor breakdowns as player-reported rather than authoritative.

There is no documented list of every visitor type, no official anomaly name list, and no published ending list. Anyone presenting a complete catalogue is working from their own play sessions. If you want to verify a claim, load the game and watch the same visitor behaviour yourself, or check the official page for updated descriptions.

## Cameras, Listening and the Cost of Guessing Wrong

Two of the advertised features are deliberately low-tech. Listening costs nothing and cannot be faked by the game — the audio is right there. Cameras give you a second angle, but the official page does not specify how many cameras exist, where they are mounted, or whether they have a cooldown. Those details are simply not documented.

That uncertainty is the point. Home Alone Anomalies is a game about acting on incomplete information. A table helps show what each signal actually gives you:

| Signal | What it provides | Documented? |
|---|---|---|
| Visitor dialogue | The visitor's stated reason for being there | Yes — official page says to listen |
| Camera check | Visual context beyond the door view | Yes — official page lists cameras |
| Porch light off | Your response when something seems wrong | Yes — official page lists it |
| Specific visitor list | Which visitors are safe or unsafe | Not documented |
| Camera count or placement | How many feeds you can check | Not documented |

Reputable third-party guides describe players combining dialogue and camera checks before deciding, which matches the official framing. They do not agree on a universal "correct" answer for every visitor, which is a reasonable sign that the game is designed around judgment rather than memorization.

## Playing With Friends and the Troll Item Angle

The official page mentions troll items for friends, and the 18-player server cap suggests the game is built for social sessions as much as solo tension. In practice that means a friend can be a second set of eyes on the door, or the reason your night goes sideways. Community discussion of the game frequently centers on private servers for exactly this reason.

What is not documented: whether troll items are purchasable, free, or dropped; whether they work in public servers; and whether they affect the cash you earn for surviving. The official description only confirms they exist as a feature aimed at friends.

## What Is Not Documented and How to Check

To be clear about the gaps, the following are not supported by the official page or reliable guides: a full visitor list, official anomaly names, house layout maps, a complete ending list, code strings, hotkeys, drop rates, and patch notes beyond the UPDATE 12 summary. Roblox experiences change frequently, and a game with this visit count gets adjusted often.

The reliable verification method is boring but effective: open the official page, read the current description, and compare it with what you see in-game. The description is the developer's own statement. A wiki, a YouTube breakdown, or a search snippet is not.
