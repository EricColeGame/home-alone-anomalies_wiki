The premise of **Home Alone Anomalies** is simple enough to explain in one sentence: you are home alone, people keep showing up at your door, and not all of them are telling the truth. That simplicity is exactly why the game has spread so quickly. There is no combat system to master and no long tutorial to sit through — just a door, a porch light, some cameras, and a decision that has to be made again and again until morning.

This guide sticks to what the developers actually advertise, plus clearly labeled observations from third-party guides, and it flags the parts of the game that simply are not documented anywhere reliable yet.

## What the Official Page Actually Says You Do

The Roblox listing for [Home Alone Anomalies](https://www.roblox.com/games/87468080405188/Home-Alone-Anomalies) describes a loop built around four actions:

- **Check the cameras** to see what is happening outside.
- **Listen to what visitors say** when they arrive.
- **Open the door** if a visitor genuinely needs help.
- **Turn off the porch light** if something about the situation is off.

Surviving the night is the win condition, and it pays out cash. The page also mentions troll items designed to be used on friends, which tells you the developers expect people to play this in groups and mess with each other as much as they play it seriously.

That is the entire advertised ruleset. Notice what is *not* there: no list of named anomalies, no visitor roster, no ending breakdown, no code system. If you see a guide claiming to have the complete official anomaly list, treat it as community guesswork rather than developer documentation.

## Reading Visitors: Reasoning, Not a Cheat Sheet

The core skill in Home Alone Anomalies is judgment under mild pressure. The official description gives you the tools — cameras, audio, the door, the porch light — but it does not tell you what any specific visitor will do. That gap is intentional, and it is where the game lives.

A reasonable approach, based on what the page documents:

**Use the camera before you use the door.** The camera exists so you can gather information without committing. If the official loop lists cameras first, that ordering is a hint about priority.

**Treat the porch light as a signal, not a decoration.** The page specifically describes turning it *off* when something is off. That suggests the light plays a role in how the situation resolves, though the exact mechanic is not documented.

**Weight what visitors say, but do not trust it blindly.** The description says to listen to what they say and to open the door *if they need help*. Both halves matter. Listening is the input; helping is the conditional output.

**Accept that mistakes are the point.** This is a survival game with a cash reward for lasting the night. If every visitor were readable at a glance, there would be no game.
