If you have played one anomaly-spotting game on Roblox, you probably assume you know how Home Alone Anomalies works. You would be half right. This experience keeps the observation-and-judgment loop that makes the genre tense, but it swaps the empty hallway for a front door, and it swaps "spot the difference" for "decide whether to let someone in." That single change reframes everything: the threat is not a shifted painting or a duplicated chair, it is a person asking you for help while you stand on the other side of a locked door.

The official Roblox page for [Home Alone Anomalies](https://www.roblox.com/games/87468080405188/Home-Alone-Anomalies) frames the premise directly. You are home alone, visitors keep arriving, and not everyone is who they claim to be. Your job is to sort the genuine callers from the impostors and make it to morning. It is created by the groups 3 AM Productions and Freeground, supports up to 18 players per server, and had accumulated roughly 208 million visits as of the official record retrieved on October 14, 2026. It is a different experience from Anomaly Watch, despite the shared vocabulary, and mixing the two up will send you looking for mechanics that do not exist here.

## What the official page actually advertises

The developer description is unusually specific about the core loop, and it is worth treating as the authority rather than relying on secondhand summaries. It lists checking cameras, listening to what visitors say, opening the door when someone needs help, and turning off the porch light when something feels off. Surviving the night earns cash, and there are troll items designed to be used on friends. That is the whole advertised skeleton: observe, listen, judge, act, repeat until dawn.

Two design details stand out. First, the porch light is a real decision variable, not decoration. Turning it off is presented as the correct response when something is wrong, which means the light itself functions as a signal you control rather than a fixed piece of scenery. Second, the page explicitly warns not everyone is who they claim to be. That wording matters: the game is telling you that a visitor's stated identity is not proof of anything, so the verification work falls on you.

The page also documents a current update. UPDATE 12, dated October 10, 2026, is a Halloween event featuring collectible pumpkins and a Halloween Pass, costumed visitors, new pets (Black Cat, White Cat, and Crawler Cat), a Top 100 pumpkin leaderboard with a Celestial Cat reward, plus bug fixes. If you are playing during the event window, expect the visitor pool to look different from the baseline version, because costumed arrivals are part of the advertised event content.

## The porch light rule, and why it is the crux

Most genre games punish you for missing a detail. Here, the porch light gives you an active tool, and the official wording implies a simple default: if a visitor seems legitimate and needs help, you open the door; if something is off, you kill the light. The hard part is that "something is off" is deliberately vague in the official material. The developers do not publish a checklist of tells, and no reputable source I can rely on provides one either.

That omission is not a gap you should try to fill with invented rules. If you see a guide claiming a definitive list of anomaly names, house layouts, or exact visitor behaviors, treat it as a third-party observation at best. Third-party guides can be genuinely useful for learning patterns, but they are not developer documentation and they go stale after updates. The honest position is that the official page tells you the verbs (check cameras, listen, open, turn off the light) and leaves the judgment criteria for you to learn by playing.

What you can do is play the loop deliberately. Use the cameras before you touch the door, so you are not deciding based on a voice alone. Listen to the full line of dialogue rather than reacting to the first word. Then make one call: door or darkness. If you are wrong, you learn something specific about what that visitor type does, which is more valuable than memorizing someone else's list.

## Cameras, dialogue, and the cash loop

The camera step exists to make the door decision informed rather than blind. In practice that means the cameras are your first pass and the door is your commitment. The official page pairs cameras with listening, which suggests the two are meant to be used together: what you see outside and what you hear from the visitor should corroborate each other. When they do not line up, that mismatch is your cue.

Surviving the night pays cash, per the official description. The page does not state amounts, multipliers, or what cash buys, so anyone quoting a specific figure is either datamining without attribution or guessing. The safe assumption is that cash is a progression currency tied to how many nights you survive, and that consistency matters more than any single lucky run.

The multiplayer ceiling of 18 players per server is worth noting because it shapes the social texture. With that many people in a server, the experience is not purely solitary dread; you will see other players moving through the same night. Troll items, which the page describes as usable on friends, confirm that the developers intend a certain amount of comedic interference alongside the tension. If you are playing with a group, expect someone to use one on you, and do not read it as a bug.

## The Halloween update and what it changes

UPDATE 12, dated October 10, 2026, is the most recent documented change. Costumed visitors are the other meaningful change. If the Halloween event is live, some arrivals will be in costume, which likely complicates the identity question at the heart of the game. Whether that makes them easier or harder to read is not documented, and it is the kind of thing you should verify yourself in a live server rather than trusting a summary.

## FAQ

**Is Home Alone Anomalies the same as Anomaly Watch?**
No. They are separate Roblox experiences with similar names. The official page for this one describes a home-alone premise with visitors and a porch light, not the anomaly-spotting format of Anomaly Watch.

**How many players can join one server?**
Up to 18, according to the official Roblox record.

**Does the porch light have to be on or off at specific times?**
The official page only says to turn it off when something is off. It does not publish a timing table or a list of conditions.

**Are there promo codes?**
The official page documents no codes. Treat any code list as unverified until you see it on the official page.

**What are the new pets in UPDATE 12?**
The Black Cat, White Cat, and Crawler Cat, introduced as part of the Halloween event. The page does not describe gameplay effects for them.

**Can I guarantee I will survive every night?**
No article can promise that, and neither can any guide. The game is built around judgment calls on incomplete information, so mistakes are part of the design.
