"Home Alone: Anomalies" is a Roblox social horror experience built around a simple premise with a nasty edge: you are home alone, visitors keep arriving, and not everyone at the door is telling the truth. The [official Roblox page](https://www.roblox.com/games/87468080405188/Home-Alone-Anomalies) for the experience credits the groups 3 AM Productions and Freeground, supports up to 18 players per server, and has drawn roughly 208 million visits as of October 14, 2026. This article explains how the loop is advertised, how community guides describe reading visitors, and where the documented information genuinely runs out.

## What the game actually asks you to do

The official description is unusually clear about the core loop. You are home alone, visitors arrive at the door, and you must decide who to trust in order to survive the night. The advertised actions are:

- Check the cameras.
- Listen to what visitors say.
- Open the door if they need help.
- Turn off the porch light if something is off.
- Survive the night to earn cash.
- Use troll items on friends.

That is the whole premise in six lines, and it is worth reading carefully because it frames the game as a judgment test rather than a reflex test. Nothing in the official description promises combat, running, or hiding in the traditional horror sense. The porch light and the door are the two levers you control, and the cameras plus dialogue are the two information sources you are given.

A useful note on identity: this is not "Anomaly Watch" or any other Roblox anomaly-spotting game. If you search for the wrong title you will land in a completely different experience with different rules. The link above is the correct place to verify anything before you commit time to it.

## The door, the light, and the honesty test

The official wording splits visitor outcomes into two categories without naming them. Someone at the door either "needs help," in which case opening is the advertised right call, or "something is off," in which case the porch light is your tool. That structure implies a binary decision per visitor, at least on the surface.

Community guides and player discussion go further, describing visitors whose dialogue does not match their appearance, or who behave inconsistently with what a genuine person would say. Treat these as third-party observations rather than developer documentation. They are consistent with the official description, but the official page does not publish a visitor list, anomaly names, or a definitive rule set for judging each arrival.

| Signal | Officially advertised | What community guides add (unverified by devs) |
|---|---|---|
| Cameras | Yes, checking them is a listed action | Used to spot visitors before the door |
| Dialogue | Yes, listening is a listed action | Mismatched or odd lines may indicate a threat |
| Porch light | Yes, turn off "if something is off" | Treated as the primary defensive switch |
| Door | Yes, open "if they need help" | Wrong opens are described as fatal or costly |
| Punishment details | Not documented | Specific penalties vary by report |

What is safe to say is that the game rewards patience. Rushing the door before checking a camera or finishing a visitor's dialogue removes the information the design hands you, which is the opposite of how the loop is advertised.

## Things the official page does not tell you

This is where most guides get into trouble, so here is a plain list of what is not documented:

- No full visitor roster, no anomaly names, and no per-visitor rule table are published by the developers.
- No code strings or promotional codes appear on the official page. If a site lists working codes for this experience, treat that claim as unverified; the developers document none.
- No house layout maps, ending lists, or hotkey bindings are officially provided.
- No drop rates, cash amounts per night, or pet acquisition odds are stated.
- No patch notes archive exists beyond the description summary of UPDATE 12.

If you need any of this, the honest method is in-game testing: play a night, watch which actions produce a survive outcome, and check the official page again after updates. Cross-reference at least two independent player reports before treating anything as settled fact.

## A practical routine for a night

Based on what the developers do advertise, a defensible order of operations looks like this:

1. Start the night by orienting to the camera feed before answering anything.
2. When a visitor arrives, hold the door and finish the dialogue. Listening is a listed mechanic for a reason.
3. Compare what you see to what you hear. If they do not match, the porch light is the advertised response.
4. Open only when the visitor's request for help reads as genuine.
5. If you are unsure, the cost of waiting is usually lower than the cost of a wrong open.

This is reasoning from the documented design, not a guarantee. Visitor behaviour is intentionally ambiguous, and the game exists to punish overconfidence as much as carelessness.

The advertised action is turning off the porch light. Listening to the visitor first is the advertised way to gather that evidence.
