Most horror games on Roblox hand you a flashlight and a monster. Home Alone: Anomalies hands you a door and a decision. You are by yourself for the night, visitors keep showing up, and the game's own description frames the whole loop as a trust problem: not everyone at your door is who they claim to be. The official [Home Alone: Anomalies](https://www.roblox.com/games/87468080405188/Home-Alone-Anomalies) page is the authority here, and it is unusually clear about intent while staying quiet about specifics — which matters, because a lot of what circulates about this experience online is guesswork.

## What the official Roblox page actually confirms

The place record (ID 87468080405188) credits two groups as creators: 3 AM Productions and Freeground. The experience was created on May 4, 2026, last updated October 10, 2026, supports up to 18 players per server, and sits at roughly 208 million visits as of the October 14, 2026 retrieval. That visit figure is the single strongest signal that this is a live, heavily played experience rather than a niche experiment.

The advertised gameplay loop, in the developers' own words, involves:

- Checking cameras
- Listening to what visitors say
- Opening the door if a visitor needs help
- Turning off the porch light if something seems off
- Surviving the night to earn cash
- Troll items for friends

That is the whole documented spine of the game. Notice how much it leaves out. There is no official list of visitor archetypes, no published anomaly taxonomy, no house layout diagram, no ending chart, and no code list. If you see a guide claiming to have the "complete" anything, treat it as third-party interpretation rather than developer documentation.

## The trust mechanic, explained without inventing visitors

The clever part of the design is that the official description never tells you which visitors are dangerous. It only tells you the two levers you control: the door and the porch light. That means the game's core skill is inference under time pressure. A visitor's dialogue is evidence. Camera feeds are evidence. The porch light is a signal you send or withhold.

What is *not* documented is the actual roster of visitors and which ones are hostile. Community guides describe the general pattern — some arrivals are genuine and asking for help, others are impersonating someone trustworthy — but the official page does not name them, and neither should anyone writing about it casually. If you want the specifics, the reliable method is to play and log what you observe, or check the game's own in-experience UI for any updated visitor or anomaly information. Do not trust a static list from a random article, including this one.

A useful mental model: treat every knock as a hypothesis you have to falsify, not a fact you accept. The developers designed the loop around checking cameras *and* listening, which suggests neither channel alone is meant to be sufficient. Players who lean on one and ignore the other are skipping half the intended information.

## Separating sources before you trust a guide

The biggest practical hazard with this experience is name collision. There is a separate Roblox game called **Anomaly Watch**, and it is a different thing entirely. Articles that blend the two will describe mechanics that do not exist here.

Third-party guides on Home Alone: Anomalies are legitimate as community observations — they can describe what players have seen — but they are not developer documentation. When a guide and the official page disagree, the official page wins. When a guide asserts something the official page is silent on, label it as unverified and test it yourself.

## How to verify anything you read

Because the game updates frequently (the record shows an update as recent as October 10, 2026), any detail can go stale. Verification is straightforward:

1. Open the official Roblox page and read the current description, not a cached summary.
2. Check the "last updated" field against the date of whatever guide you are reading.
3. In game, use the camera and dialogue systems to confirm behavior yourself rather than relying on a checklist.
4. For event items, check the in-experience Halloween Pass and leaderboard for current requirements.

What you should *not* do is assume promo codes exist. The official page documents none, and the presence of a cash-earning survival loop does not imply a code system. Do not enter codes you find on aggregator sites.
