"Home Alone Anomalies" is a Roblox experience built by the groups 3 AM Productions and Freeground, and it turns a familiar horror premise into a social judgment game. You are home alone. Visitors keep arriving at the door. Some of them need help, and some of them are not who they claim to be. Your job is to decide who to trust, survive until morning, and earn cash for making it through the night. The official experience page is at [Home Alone Anomalies](https://www.roblox.com/games/87468080405188/Home-Alone-Anomalies) on Roblox.

That premise sounds simple, but the tension comes from small decisions repeated under pressure: flipping a light, reading a camera feed, and choosing whether to open a door. This article sticks to what the official Roblox record documents and clearly labels anything that comes from third-party guides or player reports.

## What the Official Page Confirms

The official record retrieved on October 14, 2026 lists 3 AM Productions and Freeground as the creators. The place was created on May 4, 2026, and last updated on October 10, 2026. Servers support up to 18 players, and the experience has accumulated roughly 208 million visits.

The official description outlines the core loop directly:

- Check the cameras.
- Listen to what visitors say.
- Open the door if a visitor needs help.
- Turn off the porch light if something seems off.
- Survive the night to earn cash.
- Use troll items on friends.

## How the Core Loop Actually Plays

The advertised steps form a decision chain, and each step builds on the last.

Start with the cameras. Camera feeds give you context before anyone knocks, so you are not making a blind call. Then listen. The official page specifically says to listen to what visitors say, which implies dialogue matters as much as appearance. A visitor's claim is information, not proof.

Then the two decisive actions: open the door, or turn off the porch light. The page attaches a condition to each. Open the door if the visitor needs help. Turn off the porch light if something is off. That pairing suggests the porch light functions as a signal or a way to disengage, not just decoration.

Finally, surviving the night earns cash. The page does not state amounts, multipliers, or whether cash carries between sessions. Treat any figure you see elsewhere as unverified unless the developers post it.

Two other things are simply undocumented: a complete visitor list, and a full set of anomaly types. Players can help themselves by testing in-game and reading the official description after each update, since the creators do post patch summaries like UPDATE 12.

## Practical Reasoning for New Players

Because the official page emphasizes judgment rather than reflexes, approach your first sessions as observation rather than scoring.

Check cameras before answering anything. Watch a visitor's approach and note anything inconsistent with their story. Listen fully before deciding, since dialogue is listed as a core input. Use the porch light as your escape valve when something reads wrong. Open the door when the visitor genuinely needs help, as the page frames that as the helpful choice.

Then track your outcomes across several nights. This experience supports up to 18 players, so behavior in a crowded server may differ from a quiet one — but the official page does not document that difference, so treat it as something to test, not a fact.

Finally, remember the troll items the page mentions. They are aimed at friends, which tells you the experience is designed to be social and a little chaotic. Nothing here guarantees survival or a specific payout; the honest summary is that the official page gives you the loop, and the rest you learn by playing.
