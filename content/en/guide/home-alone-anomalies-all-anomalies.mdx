"Home Alone Anomalies" is a Roblox experience built on a simple but stressful premise: you are home alone for the night, visitors keep showing up at your door, and not all of them are telling the truth. It is a round-based horror game about judgment under pressure, not a jump-scare gauntlet you can brute-force. If you are searching for how the game actually works before you spend a night getting it wrong, this guide covers what the official page advertises, what third-party guides describe, and where the documentation simply runs out.

You can find the experience here: [Home Alone Anomalies on Roblox](https://www.roblox.com/games/87468080405188/Home-Alone-Anomalies).

## What the Official Page Actually Documents

The Roblox listing is the authority here. It credits two creator groups, 3 AM Productions and Freeground, with the place created on May 4, 2026 and last updated October 10, 2026. Servers support up to 18 players, and the experience has accumulated roughly 208 million visits as of the official record retrieved October 14, 2026.

The advertised loop is short and specific. Players are home alone while visitors arrive at the door. Not everyone is who they claim to be. The official description lists these actions and systems:

- Checking cameras
- Listening to what visitors say
- Opening the door if they need help
- Turning off the porch light if something is off
- Surviving the night to earn cash
- Troll items for friends

That is the entire advertised mechanical vocabulary. Notably, the official page does not name specific visitors, does not publish an anomaly list, does not describe a house layout, does not list endings, and does not mention promo codes. If you see a guide claiming a complete roster of visitors or a secret code string, treat that as speculation rather than developer documentation.

The most recent update on the listing is UPDATE 12, dated October 10, 2026, a Halloween event that adds collectible pumpkins, a Halloween Pass, costumed visitors, and new pets (Black Cat, White Cat, Crawler Cat). It also advertises a Top 100 pumpkin leaderboard with a Celestial Cat reward, alongside bug fixes.

## The Core Decision: Door, Light, or Nothing

The game's tension comes from a small number of choices repeated under time pressure. Based on the official description and third-party guide coverage, the decision flow looks roughly like this:

| Situation | Advertised action | Reasoning |
|---|---|---|
| Visitor asks for help | Open the door | The page frames door-opening as the correct response to genuine need |
| Something feels wrong | Turn off the porch light | The light is presented as the signal mechanism for suspicious arrivals |
| You want more information | Check cameras | Cameras let you verify before committing |
| You want more information | Listen to the visitor | Dialogue is a clue source, not decoration |

The important thing is that two of these four actions are information-gathering, and two are commitments. Checking cameras and listening cost you time but preserve your options. Opening the door and killing the porch light are decisions that can end your night if you read the situation wrong.

Third-party guides generally frame the porch light as a safe, non-confrontational "decline" signal — you send the visitor away without letting them inside. They also consistently describe dialogue as containing inconsistencies you can detect by paying attention, rather than randomized noise. Both of these are community observations, not statements the official page makes, so verify them in a live round before you build a strategy around them.

## Reading Visitors Without a Published Anomaly List

Because no official anomaly list exists, you cannot memorize your way through Home Alone Anomalies the way you might in a pattern-recognition game. What you can do is build a comparison habit.

Listen to a visitor's claim first, then check the camera. A visitor who describes themselves in a way that doesn't match what the camera shows is the archetypal "not who they claim to be" case the official page warns about. Costumed visitors in the current Halloween event complicate this further, since the event explicitly adds visitors in costume — so appearance alone is a weaker signal during the event than it is outside it.

Be careful not to overfit to one tell. The official page deliberately keeps visitor identity vague, and third-party guides disagree on specifics. If you find yourself treating a single dialogue line as a guaranteed anomaly marker, you are probably working from a guide that invented more certainty than the game provides.

## Multiplayer, Cash, and Troll Items

Servers hold up to 18 players, which matters more than it first appears. In a full lobby you are not the only one making trust calls, and you cannot fully control what other players do at the door or with the porch light. The official page does not document any voting system, shared decision mechanic, or role assignment, so how multiplayer rounds actually distribute those decisions is not documented — check the in-game interface to see whether other players share control of the door and light.

Surviving the night earns cash, per the official listing. The page does not publish prices, payout amounts, or what the cash is spent on. It also lists "troll items for friends" as a feature without naming any items or their effects. Any guide that lists specific troll item names or prices is going beyond the verified record.

The Halloween event's pumpkin collectibles and Top 100 leaderboard are the only progression-style rewards the official page confirms. The Celestial Cat is the named reward for the leaderboard, and the three named pets are Black Cat, White Cat, and Crawler Cat. No drop rates or unlock conditions are published.

Two habits will keep you accurate. First, open the official page and read the current description — creators update it, and UPDATE 12 landed only days before this article.
