Most horror games on Roblox ask you to run, hide, or fight. Home Alone: Anomalies, the experience published by 3 AM Productions and Freeground, asks you to do something quieter and arguably harder: stand at a door, listen to a stranger, and decide whether to open it. The official Roblox page describes a night shift of sorts — you are home alone, visitors keep arriving, and not everyone at the door is who they claim to be. Your job is to survive until morning.

That premise is simple, but the decisions inside it are not. This guide breaks down what the official page actually documents, what third-party guides have observed, and what you should simply verify yourself before trusting anyone's advice.

## What the official page actually says

According to the official Roblox record for [Home Alone: Anomalies](https://www.roblox.com/games/87468080405188/Home-Alone-Anomalies), retrieved on October 14, 2026, the experience was created by the groups 3 AM Productions and Freeground. The place was created on May 4, 2026, and last updated on October 10, 2026. It supports up to 18 players per server and has roughly 208 million visits.

The official description lists a specific loop of actions:

- Check the cameras.
- Listen to what visitors say.
- Open the door if they need help.
- Turn off the porch light if something is off.
- Survive the night to earn cash.

The same description mentions troll items intended for friends and confirms that the game is not the separate Roblox title Anomaly Watch. That distinction matters, because search results for "anomaly" games on Roblox blend together quickly. If a guide describes scanning a hallway for shifting props with a checklist, that is a different game.

## The core judgment call: door or no door

Every visitor presents the same problem in a different costume. Someone knocks, states a reason to be let in, and waits. You have two tools that the official page names directly: the cameras and the porch light. The camera gives you a second look at the person at your door. The porch light gives you a way to signal that you are not comfortable — the official wording is to turn it off "if something is off."

The reasoning here is straightforward. A visitor's claim and a visitor's appearance are two separate pieces of evidence, and the game appears designed around the gap between them. If both line up, opening the door fits the "open the door if they need help" instruction. If one of them contradicts the other, the porch light is your low-commitment answer.

What the official page does not do is tell you which specific visitors are safe, how many arrive per night, or what happens when you guess wrong. Those details vary by run and by update. Treat any guide that lists exact visitor names and outcomes as a third-party observation, not developer documentation, and confirm it in your own lobby.

## Cameras, audio, and the porch light

These three systems are the only mechanics the official description confirms, so it is worth being precise about what each one is for.

| Tool | Documented purpose | Practical use |
|---|---|---|
| Cameras | Check who is at the door | Get a second view before deciding |
| Visitor dialogue | Listen to what visitors say | Compare their claim against what you see |
| Porch light | Turn off if something is off | Decline without opening the door |
| Surviving the night | Earns cash | Repeat nights to build up currency |
| Troll items | For friends | Social pranks, not survival tools |

Notice what is missing from that table: no health system, no stamina meter, no inventory of defensive weapons, and no documented list of anomaly types. If you see a guide describing, say, a specific number of hearts or a named "entity" with statistics, that information is not in the official description and may be community speculation, an older build, or content from a different game entirely.

The safest habit is to lean on audio. Visitor dialogue is the one signal the developers chose to advertise, which suggests it carries real weight. Listening carefully costs you nothing and gives you a claim to test against the camera feed.

## The Halloween update and what it added

The official description includes UPDATE 12, dated October 10, 2026, which introduced a Halloween event. Documented elements include collectible pumpkins, a Halloween Pass, costumed visitors, and three new pets: Black Cat, White Cat, and Crawler Cat. There is also a Top 100 pumpkin leaderboard, with a Celestial Cat listed as a reward, plus bug fixes.

Two cautions here. First, "costumed visitors" adds a wrinkle to the judgment loop — a costume can change how someone looks without changing who they are, which is exactly the kind of ambiguity the game is built around. Second, the official page documents no promo codes. If a video or wiki page claims a working code, treat it as unverified and check the official page before spending time on it.

## What is not documented and how to check

Plenty of reasonable questions have no answer in the official record:

- How many nights or shifts exist
- Whether there are multiple endings
- A complete visitor roster or anomaly list
- Any code strings, hotkeys, or console commands
- Exact cash payouts or shop prices
- Drop rates for pets or pumpkins

None of that is confirmed. The honest way to resolve those questions is to play the experience and read the description on the [official Roblox page](https://www.roblox.com/games/87468080405188/Home-Alone-Anomalies), which the developers update as patches land. Community wikis and YouTube guides can be genuinely useful for tactics, but they age fast and are written by players, not by 3 AM Productions or Freeground.

## A reasonable way to approach each night

Start by treating the first few visitors as calibration. Watch the camera, listen to the dialogue, and note whether the two agree. Use the porch light as your default when you are unsure, since it costs you a visitor rather than your run. Open the door when a visitor's story, appearance, and tone all point the same direction. Then reset, because the next visitor is a fresh puzzle.

This is a game about restraint more than reflexes. Nothing in the official description promises that caution always works, and no guide can guarantee you reach morning. What the documented features do give you is a consistent method: look, listen, decide, and use the light when the answer is not clear.
