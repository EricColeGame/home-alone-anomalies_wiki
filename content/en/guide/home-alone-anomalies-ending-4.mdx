## Cameras, dialogue, and the porch light in practice

Because the official page does not explain how anomalies behave, the most honest thing a guide can do is describe the reasoning rather than invent a monster manual.

Cameras exist so you can compare what you see with what you hear. If a visitor's claim and the camera view disagree, that disagreement is your signal. This is the standard logic of the genre, and it is consistent with the official description, but the game's specific camera layout and any image distortion are not documented. Learn them in a live server rather than trusting a list.

Dialogue exists so you can be lied to. Listening is listed as a core action, which strongly implies that the content of what a visitor says matters, not just whether a visitor appeared. Again, no official list of visitors or their lines exists, so any "all visitor types" chart you find is a community artifact and may be outdated after Update 12.

The porch light is the one action that flips a physical condition. The official phrasing is conditional: turn it off if something is off. That suggests the light is meant to be a response to a specific read, not a permanent setting. Whether leaving it on has a cost is not documented. The safe habit is to treat the light as a deliberate choice each time a visitor arrives, and to watch whether outcomes change when you toggle it.

One practical tip that follows from the official description rather than from rumor: make your decision in a fixed order. Check the camera first, then audio, then decide on the door and the light. A repeatable sequence prevents panic decisions, which is the actual failure mode in this kind of game.
