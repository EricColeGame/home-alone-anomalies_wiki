Most horror games on Roblox hand you a flashlight and a dark hallway. Home Alone: Anomalies hands you a door. You are a kid left at home for the night, and people keep knocking. Some of them genuinely need help. Some of them are wearing a friendly voice like a costume. The entire experience is built around that single, uncomfortable question, and everything else in the game exists to make the question harder to answer.

This article sticks to what the [official Roblox page for Home Alone: Anomalies](https://www.roblox.com/games/87468080405188/Home-Alone-Anomalies) documents and what reputable third-party guides have observed, and it flags clearly where the record runs out. ## What the official page actually advertises

According to the official Roblox record from October 14, 2026, Home Alone: Anomalies is created by the groups 3 AM Productions and Freeground. The place was created on May 4, 2026, last updated October 10, 2026, supports up to 18 players per server, and sits at roughly 208 million visits. That last number tells you something useful before you ever load in: this is a game with a very large, very active audience, which means the community has collectively tested a lot of door decisions.

The official description frames the core loop directly. You are home alone. Visitors keep arriving. Not everyone is who they claim to be. You check cameras, you listen to what visitors say, you open the door if they need help, and you turn off the porch light if something is off. Survive the night and you earn cash. The description also mentions troll items you can use on friends, which is worth remembering the next time a stranger asks to come inside.

That is the whole advertised premise. Notice what is *not* there: no list of named anomalies, no documented endings, no published house layout, no stated cash values, and no promo codes. The official page documents none, so any article claiming to hand you a code list is guessing or padding.

## The four tools the game gives you

The official description names four actions, and they map cleanly onto a decision process rather than a reaction test.

- **Cameras.** These let you observe before you commit. A visitor's appearance at the door and their appearance on camera are two separate pieces of evidence.
- **Listening.** What a visitor says is information. The description treats speech as something you evaluate, not something you trust by default.
- **Opening the door.** This is the help action. It is also the risk action. The description links opening specifically to visitors who *need help*, which implies the door is not the correct response to every knock.
- **The porch light.** Turning it off is the described response when something is off. It is a refusal signal, not an attack.

In practice, most of your night is spent comparing those four outputs against each other. A voice that sounds distressed plus a camera view that looks wrong is a contradiction, and contradictions are the point.

## Reading a visitor without a checklist

Search results for this game are full of "all anomalies" lists, and that is exactly where third-party content gets shaky. Reputable third-party guides describe the general shape of the game, but a full roster of visitors and anomaly names is not part of the official documentation, and the current Halloween content means any list published before October 10, 2026 is already incomplete. Treat circulating lists as community observations with an expiration date, not as developer documentation.

The more durable skill is reasoning under uncertainty:

1. **Get a second opinion before you act.** The cameras and the porch view are not the same source. If they disagree, you have found something.
2. **Weigh speech against behavior.** A calm voice is not proof of safety, and an unsettling one is not proof of threat. The description mentions listening specifically because the content of what is said matters.
3. **Remember the light is reversible.** Turning the porch light off is described as a response to something being off, not a commitment. Nothing in the official material suggests you are locked out of a correct decision afterward.
4. **Do not confuse this with Anomaly Watch.** Anomaly Watch is a different Roblox game with a different structure. Advice, codes, or mechanic lists for that game do not transfer here.

## Playing with friends, and playing fair

The official description mentions troll items for friends. In a game where trust is the mechanic, using an item on a friend is thematically perfect and socially risky. If you are playing in a group, the practical approach is to set expectations before the round starts, because the game already makes cooperation hard by design.

Also remember that servers hold up to 18 players. That is a large enough group that you may be sharing a night with people whose only goal is to make your decisions worse. The official material does not document a reporting or role system, so use Roblox's standard tools if someone crosses a line.
