Home Alone Anomalies is a Roblox experience built on a simple but tense premise: you are alone in a house, the doorbell keeps ringing, and not every visitor is telling the truth. The official page describes a loop of checking cameras, listening to what visitors say, opening the door when someone genuinely needs help, and turning off the porch light when something feels off. Survive the night, and you earn cash.

That description is deliberately short, and that matters. Much of what circulates about the game—visitor names, exact anomaly types, endings, layouts—comes from player discussion rather than the developers. This article separates what the creators actually advertise from what the community has observed, and points out where players should simply check the game itself.

The experience is published by the groups 3 AM Productions and Freeground. According to the official Roblox record retrieved October 14, 2026, the place was created May 4, 2026, was last updated October 10, 2026, supports up to 18 players per server, and has around 208 million visits. The official page is the authority here: [Home Alone: Anomalies on Roblox](https://www.roblox.com/games/87468080405188/Home-Alone-Anomalies).

## What the Official Page Actually Advertises

The advertised feature set is narrow and mechanical:

- **Cameras** — you can check cameras, which suggests the front of the house and the approach to the door are observable without opening anything.
- **Listening** — what visitors say at the door is part of your information. Dialogue is a signal, not decoration.
- **Opening the door for people who need help** — helping is framed as a legitimate action, not a trap by default.
- **Turning off the porch light when something is off** — this is the game's stated rejection or suspicion action.
- **Surviving the night for cash** — cash is the stated reward for a successful night.
- **Troll items for friends** — explicitly social, opt-in messing with other players.

What the page does **not** document: a complete visitor roster, anomaly names, a house map, specific endings, or any promo codes. The official record lists no codes, so treat any "active code" list as unverified.

## Reading Visitors Instead of Memorizing Lists

New players often search for a list of "correct" answers. That is the wrong instinct for two reasons.

First, no developer-published list exists. The official description gives you the decision framework—help, or turn off the light—but not a table of who is safe. Second, games of this genre are updated frequently, and Update 12 explicitly added costumed visitors, which means the visitor pool is not fixed.

What you can reasonably rely on is the framework itself:

| Signal | Advertised reading | Why it matters |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Camera view of the approach | Compare what you see with what is claimed | Visual mismatches are the core tension |
| What the visitor says | Dialogue is information | Requests for help are supposedly the door-opening case |
| Your instinct that something is off | Turn off the porch light | The game's stated rejection action |

That table is a restatement of the official loop, not a tier list. The specific tells—wrong voice, wrong silhouette, wrong wording—are community observations and shift between updates.

## Update 12 and the Halloween Event

The official description lists **UPDATE 12 (10.10.2026)**, a Halloween event. The documented contents are:

- Collectible pumpkins
- A Halloween Pass
- Costumed visitors
- New pets: **Black Cat, White Cat, and Crawler Cat**
- A Top 100 pumpkin leaderboard with a **Celestial Cat** reward
- Bug fixes

Note the implication: costumed visitors arrived with a seasonal event, so a visitor behaving unusually may simply be dressed for Halloween rather than being an anomaly. The official page does not explain how to distinguish the two, and it does not publish pumpkin spawn locations, pass pricing, or leaderboard cutoffs. Verify those in-game.

The three cats are the only pets named in the official record. Any other pet names you see quoted elsewhere are not supported by the source.

## Multiplayer, Troll Items, and the 18-Player Limit

The record states up to 18 players per server. That is a large group for a house-based horror game, and it shapes how the advertised "troll items for friends" feature works—these are items aimed at other players in your session, not at visitors.

This is also where expectations get muddled. Players arriving from single-player anomaly games sometimes assume a solo experience and get confused when other people are moving around the same house. If the social layer is not what you want, check the server population before joining; that is a lobby-level observation, not something the official page explains.

## Separating Documented Facts From Community Claims

Because third-party guides on this experience are secondary observation, not developer documentation, here is a clean split.

**Documented by the developers:** cameras, listening to visitors, opening the door for those who need help, turning off the porch light when something is off, surviving for cash, troll items, 18 players, Update 12 Halloween content as listed above.

**Community-observed, treat with caution:** specific anomaly behaviors, which dialogue lines are reliable, recommended night strategies, and any ranking of visitors. These change with patches and vary by server.

**Not documented at all:** promo codes, exact cash amounts, drop rates, a full anomaly index, house blueprints, and named endings. Do not trust anyone presenting these as official.

If you want to verify a claim, the fastest method is the game itself: play a night, watch the camera before you act, and note what the visitor said versus what you saw. The official Roblox page remains the place to confirm whether a feature is real.

**Are there promo codes?**
The official page documents none. Any code list you find is unverified.

**How many players per server?**
Up to 18, per the official record.

**What did Update 12 add?**
A Halloween event with collectible pumpkins, a Halloween Pass, costumed visitors, Black Cat / White Cat / Crawler Cat pets, a Top 100 pumpkin leaderboard with a Celestial Cat reward, and bug fixes.

**Should I always open the door?**
The official description ties opening the door to visitors who need help and ties turning off the porch light to something being off. That is the whole decision framework the developers published—use the camera and the dialogue, and accept that no guide can guarantee a clean night.
