If you have played any of the wave of anomaly-spotting games on Roblox, the premise of [Home Alone: Anomalies](https://www.roblox.com/games/87468080405188/Home-Alone-Anomalies) will sound familiar at first: something is subtly wrong, and you have to notice it before it notices you. What makes this experience its own thing is the framing. You are not a night-shift security guard patrolling a facility. You are a kid alone at home, and the threat arrives politely, one knock at a time.

That shift matters. In most anomaly games, the correct answer is always "report the wrong thing." Here, the deciding question is social: is the person at the door actually who they say they are, and do they actually need help? Getting that wrong in either direction has consequences.

This guide sticks to what the official Roblox page and reputable community coverage actually support, and it flags clearly where information is missing. It is not a walkthrough, and it will not promise you a clean survival every run.

## What the official page actually documents

The Roblox listing, created by the groups 3 AM Productions and Freeground (place created May 4, 2026, most recently updated October 10, 2026), describes a loop with several moving parts:

- **Checking cameras** to see what is happening outside.
- **Listening to what visitors say** before you act.
- **Opening the door if a visitor needs help.**
- **Turning off the porch light when something is off.**
- **Surviving the night to earn cash.**
- **Troll items** you can use on friends.

Servers hold up to 18 players, and the experience has accumulated roughly 208 million visits according to the official record retrieved October 14, 2026.

Two of those bullets are the entire game. Opening the door and killing the porch light are opposite actions, and you have to pick the right one per visitor. Everything else — cameras, dialogue, cash, troll items — exists to support that decision.

## The two decisions that define every visit

Think of each knock as a single binary choice with a twist: the *signals* pointing you toward the answer are imperfect, and they can conflict.

| What you observe | What it generally suggests | Caveat |
|---|---|---|
| Visitor's dialogue sounds like a genuine request for help | Consider opening the door | Some arrivals are described as costumed visitors, so appearances and claims are not automatically trustworthy |
| Camera view shows something inconsistent with the story | Consider turning off the porch light | Camera coverage and house layout are not documented publicly, so you learn sightlines by playing |
| You feel unsure | Do nothing yet and re-check | Passivity is a valid action, but the night still has to end |

The porch light is the more counterintuitive tool. Turning off a light reads like a defensive move, but the page frames it as the response for when "something is off" — meaning it is the game's way of telling an unwanted guest that the house is not open for business. If you instinctively open the door for everyone who knocks, you will learn quickly why the light exists.

A reasonable personal rule, built from the advertised mechanics rather than any hidden data: **treat dialogue as a claim, not evidence.** A visitor saying they need help is the starting point of your investigation, not the conclusion. Use the camera to test the claim, then act. If the camera cannot confirm or deny, listening again costs you nothing but time.

## How to read the Halloween update without overreading it

UPDATE 12, dated October 10, 2026, is a Halloween event, and the official description lists it in plain terms: collectible pumpkins, a Halloween Pass, costumed visitors, and new pets — Black Cat, White Cat, and Crawler Cat. There is also a Top 100 pumpkin leaderboard with a Celestial Cat as the reward.

The phrase "costumed visitors" is the interesting one. It is documented as a feature of the event, but the page does not explain whether costumes change how you should judge a knock, whether they are purely cosmetic, or whether they interact with the existing trust mechanic at all. Anyone claiming to know the exact rules is going beyond the source. Verify it yourself: play during the event, watch whether a costumed visitor's behavior differs from a normal one, and compare notes with other players in the server.

Likewise, the pets are listed by name only. The official page does not state what any pet does, how you obtain them, or what the Halloween Pass costs in Robux or time. Do not trust a guide that fills in those blanks confidently.

## Practical habits that hold up across runs

Rather than memorizing a script, build a repeatable process:

1. **Hear the visitor out first.** Dialogue is free information. Skipping it means deciding blind.
2. **Check the camera before touching the door.** This is the only advertised way to independently verify what you were told.
3. **When signals conflict, choose the light.** Turning off the porch light is the low-commitment action — it does not invite anyone in.
4. **Use your ears across the whole night.** The page emphasizes listening, which suggests audio cues matter beyond just the words spoken.
5. **Play with people.** With up to 18 players per server, you can split attention between the door and other angles, and troll items give the group something to do between visits.

A caution about community guides: many anomaly-game writeups circulate as if they were developer documentation. Treat third-party guides as observations from other players, useful for comparison but not authoritative. When a guide and the official page disagree, the official page wins.

## FAQ

**Is Home Alone: Anomalies the same as Anomaly Watch?**
No. They are separate Roblox experiences with different developers and premises. This one casts you as a kid at home deciding which visitors to trust.

**When should I open the door?**
According to the official description, when a visitor needs help. In practice, listen to them and check the cameras before committing.

**What does turning off the porch light do?**
The page lists it as the action to take when something is off. The precise mechanical effect is not documented, so observe it in-game.

**Does the game have codes?**
The official page documents none. Do not rely on code lists from third parties.

**How many players per server?**
Up to 18, per the official record.

**How do I earn cash?**
By surviving the night, per the official description. Specific payout amounts are not published.
