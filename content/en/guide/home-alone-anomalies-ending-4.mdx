## How Anomaly Detection Actually Works in Home Alone Anomalies

Every run starts from the same baseline house. Your job is to notice deviations and report them correctly. Two failure modes matter equally: **missing** a real anomaly, and **false-reporting** a normal object. Many players assume only the first one hurts them — in practice, a single bad report is often what quietly reroutes a run away from ending 4.

Learning to classify anomalies makes the whole process faster.

| Anomaly type | What it looks like | How to confirm it |
|---|---|---|
| Object swap | A prop replaced by a similar one | Compare against your baseline memory of the room |
| Position shift | Furniture nudged out of place | Check alignment against walls, rugs, and doorways |
| Lighting change | Lamp color, tint, or brightness differs | Scan ceiling fixtures and window light sources |
| Audio cue | Music, footsteps, or a voice changes | Stand still for a few seconds and listen |
| Missing item | Something that should be present is gone | Run a fixed mental checklist per room |
| Text change | Posters, notes, or labels read differently | Read every sign, even the familiar ones |

Community reports consistently suggest that lighting and audio anomalies are the most commonly missed, simply because players scan objects and forget to look up or stop moving.

## Frequently Asked Questions

**How many anomalies do I need to find for the home alone anomalies ending 4?**
Community reports suggest the requirement is close to full coverage rather than a partial threshold, but the exact number is adjusted between updates. Aim to find everything and report nothing you cannot confirm — that approach satisfies the requirement regardless of the current count.

**Can I unlock ending 4 on my first run?**
Technically yes, but practically almost no one does. You need a baseline understanding of the normal house to detect subtle swaps and lighting changes, and that knowledge usually comes from at least one completed practice run.

**Does quitting or dying reset my progress?**
In most versions, ending progress is evaluated at the end of a completed run, so quitting early can void the attempt. If you think the run is ruined, finish it anyway — you may still learn which decision point broke the route.

**Why did I get a different ending even though I found everything?**
The usual cause is a false report or a missed scripted interaction. Accuracy and choices are tracked separately, so a perfect anomaly sweep can still land you on a mid-tier ending if you skipped a required decision moment.
