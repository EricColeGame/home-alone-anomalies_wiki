## The Core Decision: Answer the Door or Not

The design asks you to make a judgment call repeatedly, and the two official verbs are the whole game. Opening the door is the compassionate option; visitors who need help presumably reward that choice. Turning off the porch light is the defensive option, signalling that you have identified something wrong.

What makes this interesting is that the verified description never promises the two options are clean opposites. "Not everyone is who they claim to be" means the same action — opening the door — can be right or catastrophic depending on context. Community guides on YouTube and fan wikis commonly describe this as a process of cross-referencing what a visitor says against what your cameras show. That framing is reasonable and consistent with the official feature list, but treat it as third-party observation rather than developer documentation: exact visitor dialogue and anomaly behaviours are not published in an official reference format.

One practical habit that follows from the official features: use the cameras before you act, and treat the audio as evidence rather than atmosphere. The listing explicitly separates "checking cameras" and "listening to what visitors say," which suggests both channels carry information. Beyond that, no source documents a guaranteed decision table, and you should be sceptical of anyone who claims one.
