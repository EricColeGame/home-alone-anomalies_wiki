"Home Alone Anomalies" is one of those Roblox titles whose name tells you almost everything and almost nothing at once. You are home alone. Something is anomalous. The rest — who knocks, what they want, and whether opening the door is mercy or a mistake — is the actual game. This article breaks down what the experience documents about itself, what players report, and what remains genuinely unverified, so you can decide how to approach a night shift at the front door.

The official experience page is the only authority worth trusting here. You can find it at [Home Alone: Anomalies on Roblox](https://www.roblox.com/games/87468080405188/Home-Alone-Anomalies), created by the groups 3 AM Productions and Freeground. ## What the Game Officially Says You Do

The official description is short and specific, which is useful. It frames the core loop as a series of judgment calls:

- Check cameras to observe what is happening outside.
- Listen to what visitors say when they arrive.
- Open the door if someone genuinely needs help.
- Turn off the porch light if something feels off.
- Survive the night to earn cash.
- Use troll items on friends.

That is the whole advertised premise. Notably, the description does not name individual visitors, does not list anomaly types, does not describe house layouts or endings, and does not publish any promo codes. If you see a list of "all anomalies" or "secret codes" floating around, treat it as unverified until you can confirm the behavior yourself in-game.

The framing matters: this is not a hide-and-seek horror game where you avoid everything. The tension comes from the fact that some arrivals are legitimate and some are not, and the game gives you tools — cameras, audio, the porch light — to make that distinction. Opening the door is not automatically wrong. It is conditionally right.

## The Trust Mechanic, and Why It Is the Whole Game

Most anomaly-spotting games on Roblox ask a single question: is this thing wrong? *Home Alone: Anomalies* asks a harder one: is this thing wrong, and does that mean I should refuse it help?

That distinction is what the official description implies by pairing "open the door if they need help" with "turn off the porch light if something is off." Two separate actions, two separate judgments. A visitor can be strange without being hostile, and a visitor can be plausible without being safe. The porch light functions as your refusal signal — a way to disengage without opening the door.

Because the official page does not document the full visitor roster or the exact consequences of each decision, any guide claiming a complete anomaly list is working from community testing, not developer documentation. That does not make it worthless — third-party guides are often accurate — but it does mean the information can go stale after an update.

## Community Observations vs. Documented Facts

This is where a lot of write-ups go wrong. Player reports and third-party guides are useful, but they are observations, not documentation. A few honest categories:

**Documented by the official page:** cameras, listening to visitors, opening the door for those who need help, the porch light toggle, surviving to earn cash, troll items, 18-player servers, the Halloween event and its listed rewards.

**Reported by third-party guides:** that certain visitors behave differently depending on costume, that specific audio cues precede specific outcomes, and that some nights escalate in difficulty. Treat these as leads to test, not rules to memorize. Behavior can shift with any patch, and this game updates regularly.

**Not documented at all:** a complete roster of visitors, named anomaly categories, house floor plans, ending types, hotkeys, drop rates, exact cash payouts, or promo codes. The official page documents no codes, so any code list you encounter is unverified by definition.

A practical way to sort signal from noise: when a guide makes a claim, ask whether it is testable in one night. "Turn off the porch light when the visitor repeats the same phrase" is testable. "This visitor is always safe" is a claim that only holds until the next update.

## How to Play Smart Without a Cheat Sheet

Because the game withholds its own answer key, the most reliable skill is consistency. Pick a decision rule and apply it every night: watch the camera first, listen before acting, and only open the door when the visitor's stated need holds up against what you observed. The porch light exists so you always have a no-cost refusal available.

Playing with others adds another layer. With up to 18 players per server, you can split camera duty and cross-check what each person heard — two listeners catch more than one. The troll items mentioned on the official page are aimed at friends, which fits the same social logic: the game is designed to be played alongside people you can argue with.

One caution worth stating plainly: no approach guarantees survival, and no guide can promise it. The official description frames the night as something you try to survive, which means failure is part of the design.
