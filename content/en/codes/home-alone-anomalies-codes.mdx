The premise of [Home Alone: Anomalies](https://www.roblox.com/games/87468080405188/Home-Alone-Anomalies) is simple enough to explain in one sentence: you are home alone, people keep knocking, and not all of them are telling the truth. That is also exactly why the experience gets confusing fast. The game gives you a porch light, cameras, and a stream of voices at the door, then leaves the judgment call to you. This guide sticks to what the creators have actually documented, adds what third-party guides consistently report, and clearly flags the parts nobody has confirmed.

A note on identity before anything else: this is the Roblox experience by the groups 3 AM Productions and Freeground (place created May 4, 2026; last updated October 10, 2026; up to 18 players per server). It is not "Anomaly Watch" or any other anomaly-spotting game with a similar name.

## What the Official Page Actually Advertises

The official Roblox description is short, and it is worth reading literally rather than through the lens of "anomaly game" expectations. It lists these actions as part of play:

- Checking the cameras
- Listening carefully to what visitors say
- Opening the door when someone genuinely needs help
- Turning off the porch light when something feels off
- Surviving the night to earn cash
- Buying troll items to use on friends

That is the core loop. Notice what is *not* in that list: no stated list of anomalies, no visitor roster, no tiered endings, no code system, and no guarantees about what happens if you get a call wrong. Anything beyond the bullets above comes from players, not the developers.

One detail worth internalizing: the porch light is described as a response to a bad feeling, not a lie detector. The official text pairs turning it off with "something is off," which suggests it is a defensive move rather than a permanent solution to every suspicious knock.

## How to Approach the Door Without Guessing Wildly

Third-party guides for this experience tend to describe the same broad rhythm: watch the camera feed, listen to the dialogue, and treat contradictions between what you see and what you hear as your main signal. That is a reasonable synthesis of the official wording, but attribute it correctly: these are community observations, not developer documentation.

A practical approach that follows from the official description:

1. **Watch first, decide second.** The camera check exists for a reason. Look before you touch the door or the light.
2. **Listen for what is being asked.** The official wording is "opening the door if they need help," which frames the decision around need, not around friendliness. A visitor who is chatty is not automatically a visitor who needs in.
3. **Use the porch light as your exit.** If something is wrong and you are not sure what, the documented response is to turn the light off. You do not have to diagnose the anomaly to react to it.
4. **Survive the night.** Cash is tied to surviving, per the official page, so a cautious night that ends in the morning beats a confident night that ends early.

What no source documents: which visitors exist, how many anomalies there are, whether specific dialogue lines are tells, what the house's rooms are, or whether there are multiple endings. If you see a confident list of anomaly names or a house map, treat it as unverified until you reproduce it in your own session.

## What to Verify Yourself, and How

Because this experience updates frequently (it went from May 2026 creation to an October 2026 event in a matter of months), any guide ages quickly. Three places beat any article:

- **The official Roblox page** for the current update label and advertised features.
- **The in-game tutorial or event menu**, which is the only place exact Halloween Pass contents and pet details are authoritative.
- **Your own recordings.** If you are going to write down what a visitor said, record the session. Memory is a bad witness in a game built on unreliable claims.

Also worth saying plainly: the official page documents no promo codes. If a site claims codes for this experience, that claim is not supported by the developer's own listing, and you can confirm that yourself by checking the page before entering anything.

No. They are separate Roblox experiences. This one is place 87468080405188 by 3 AM Productions and Freeground, built around visitors arriving at your door while you are home alone.

The official description says to turn it off "if something is off." It is presented as a reaction to suspicion, not as a labeled anomaly detector.

**How do I earn cash?**
The official description ties cash to surviving the night. No further payout formula is documented.
